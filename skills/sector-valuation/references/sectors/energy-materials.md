# Energy & Materials Playbooks

E&P (oil & gas producers) · Midstream · Refiners · Integrated majors · Oilfield services · Mining & metals · Chemicals

Commodity businesses: value = reserves/assets × (price − unit cost) over finite life. Always state the **price deck** (strip, mid-cycle, and a stress case) — a valuation without a price deck is meaningless.

---

## E&P (upstream oil & gas)

**Value equation:** NAV = PV(PDP) + risked PV(PUD) + risked PV(undeveloped inventory) − net debt − PV(G&A) − PV(asset retirement obligations) ± hedge book mark.

**KPIs:** production (boe/d) and oil cut · proved reserves (1P) split PDP/PUD · reserve life (R/P) · F&D cost per boe · lease operating expense (LOE) per boe · cash margin per boe · recycle ratio (cash margin ÷ F&D; >2x good) · reinvestment rate (capex ÷ CFO) · corporate breakeven (WTI that covers maintenance capex + base dividend) · Tier-1 drilling inventory years · hedging.

**What is disclosed (US filers):** SEC Rule 4-10 / ASC 932 supplemental data in the 10-K gives proved reserves by category, the reserve roll-forward (extensions, revisions, purchases, sales, production), costs incurred, and the **standardized measure** (after-tax PV at 10%, using trailing 12-month SEC average prices). PV-10 (pre-tax) is usually in the MD&A or investor deck. Probable/possible (2P/3P) reserves are optional for US filers and mostly not reported.

**If undisclosed:**
- F&D cost = costs incurred (exploration + development + acquisition) ÷ reserve additions (extensions + revisions + purchases), 3-yr average to smooth lumpiness.
- LOE/boe = lease operating expense ÷ production.
- Beyond proved reserves: remaining locations (investor decks) × EUR per well (peer type curves, state well data) × risk factor (Tier 1: 0.8–0.9; Tier 2/3: 0.3–0.6).
- Breakeven: (maintenance capex + LOE + G&A + interest + base dividend) ÷ production, solved for oil price.

**Benchmarks:** Permian new-well breakevens commonly cited ~$60–70 WTI (Dallas Fed Energy Survey; refresh annually); top-tier operators' corporate breakevens below that; recycle ratio >2x healthy.

**Valuation:** NAV at strip and at a mid-cycle deck (state both, e.g. $65–75 WTI / $3.5–4 HH — refresh); P/NAV vs peers; EV/DACF and FCF yield at strip and at strip −$10; NAV sensitivity per $5/bbl.

**Traps:** standardized measure uses trailing SEC prices, not forward strip; PUD 5-year development rule inflates/deflates reserves; inventory depletion hidden by acquisitions; hedge gains in earnings (R3); valuing on current production as a perpetuity.

## Midstream (pipelines, storage, processing)

**Value equation:** Contracted volumes × fee − opex; value = EBITDA × multiple justified by contract tenor, counterparty quality and growth capex returns.

**KPIs:** fee-based % of gross margin · minimum-volume commitments and contract tenor · counterparty credit · distribution/dividend coverage · leverage (net debt/EBITDA) · growth capex returns (build multiple vs trading multiple).

**Valuation:** EV/EBITDA, DCF on distributable cash flow, dividend yield vs coverage.

**Traps:** commodity-exposed processing margins labelled fee-based; volume declines in mature basins; re-contracting risk.

---

## Refiners

**Value equation:** Throughput capacity × utilization × (crack spread × capture rate) − opex.

**KPIs:** mid-cycle crack spreads (3-2-1, regional) · capture rate · utilization · Nelson complexity · RIN costs · turnaround schedule.

**Valuation:** mid-cycle EV/EBITDA; replacement cost per barrel of capacity.

**Traps:** capitalizing peak cracks; RIN/regulatory cost swings (R3); turnaround timing.

---

## Integrated majors

SOTP: upstream (NAV), downstream (mid-cycle EV/EBITDA), chemicals, low-carbon (option value or capital at cost). Key: breakeven for capex + dividend, buyback capacity at mid-cycle prices, reserve replacement.

---

## Oilfield services

**Value equation:** Rig/frac-fleet count × utilization × pricing; international/offshore cycles longer than US land.

**KPIs:** rig counts (Baker Hughes), utilization, pricing, backlog (offshore), FCF conversion.

**Valuation:** mid-cycle EV/EBITDA; replacement cost of fleets.

---

## Mining & metals

**Value equation:** NAV = PV over mine life of (production × (price − AISC or C1 cost)) − sustaining & expansion capex − closure costs, discounted at 5–8% (real, by jurisdiction risk).

**KPIs:** reserves & resources (S-K 1300 / NI 43-101 technical reports) · grade and recovery · AISC (gold) / C1 cash cost (copper) and cost-curve position · mine life · jurisdiction risk · expansion pipeline capex intensity.

**If undisclosed:** cost-curve position from WGC (gold) or industry consultants; production profiles from technical-report mine plans.

**Valuation:** P/NAV at long-term price deck (spot and consensus long-term); EV/EBITDA mid-cycle; jurisdiction discount.

**Traps:** spot-price NAV at the peak; reserve grade decline; capex overruns on new projects (common — haircut sanctioned budgets); royalty/tax regime changes.

---

## Chemicals

**Value equation:** Spread (product price − feedstock) × volume; specialty = pricing power on formulation, commodity = cycle.

**KPIs:** spreads vs feedstock (ethane vs naphtha advantage), utilization, specialty mix, volume vs price decomposition.

**Valuation:** commodity — mid-cycle EV/EBITDA, replacement cost; specialty — P/E / DCF with pricing-power evidence.

**Traps:** peak spreads; China capacity additions; destocking cycles.
