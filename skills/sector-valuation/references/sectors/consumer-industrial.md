# Consumer & Industrial Playbooks

Retail · Restaurants & franchisors · Consumer staples · Autos & EVs · Industrials & capital goods · Aerospace & defense · Airlines · Railroads · Trucking, LTL & logistics · Shipping · Space & early-stage hardware

---

## Retail (store-based)

**Value equation:** Stores × sales per store (traffic × ticket) × store margin − corporate costs; growth value = new stores × (store NPV at cost of capital).

**KPIs:** same-store sales (traffic vs ticket) · sales per sq ft · gross margin (shrink, markdowns) · inventory turns · new-store productivity and cash-on-cash return · e-commerce penetration and margin · lease obligations.

**If undisclosed:** store-level economics from investor-day slides or peer disclosures; traffic from foot-traffic panels (Placer.ai).

**Benchmarks:** inventory turns >6x (general merch varies); positive SSS in line with or above the sector; net debt (lease-adjusted)/EBITDAR <3–3.5x.

**Valuation:** EV/EBITDA (lease-adjusted, mid-cycle); store-level DCF (whitespace × unit NPV) for growers.

**Traps:** comps inflated by price inflation with falling traffic; inventory build ahead of slowdown; lease liabilities ignored in EV.

## Restaurants & franchisors

**Value equation:** Units × AUV × store margin (company-operated) or × royalty rate (franchised); growth = new units × (unit NPV).

**KPIs:** AUV · restaurant-level (4-wall) margin · new-unit cash-on-cash return (yr-2/3 EBITDA ÷ build cost) · payback · same-store sales · franchise mix · franchisee health (their margins, leverage).

**If undisclosed:** franchisors' **Franchise Disclosure Documents (FDD Item 19)** often give AUVs and unit economics; build cost from FDD Item 7.

**Benchmarks:** strong concepts: yr-2 cash-on-cash >30–40%, payback <3 years; restaurant-level margins 15–20%+.

**Valuation:** unit-economics DCF + EV/EBITDA; asset-light franchisors deserve higher multiples (royalty streams), company-operated lower.

**Traps:** new units cannibalizing old; franchisee stress hidden until closures; refranchising gains (R0).

## Consumer staples

**Value equation:** Volume × price/mix × gross margin − brand reinvestment; a bond-like stream valued against yields.

**KPIs:** organic growth decomposed into volume vs price/mix · gross margin vs input costs · A&P (advertising & promotion) % sales · market share (scanner data) · private-label share · emerging-market exposure.

**Benchmarks:** healthy organic growth 3–5% with positive volume; gross margin >35–40% for branded.

**Valuation:** DCF / DDM; P/E and FCF yield vs 10-yr Treasury and peers; EV/EBITDA.

**Traps:** price-led growth with negative volume (elasticity catching up); under-investment in brands to hit margins; GLP-1 consumption effects for snacks/beverages.

## Autos & EVs

**Value equation:** Units × ASP × auto gross margin (ex-credits) + software/services + energy/other segments.

**KPIs:** deliveries · auto GM ex-regulatory credits · cost per vehicle · capacity utilization · inventory days · regulatory-credit revenue (R3/R0) · energy storage deployments (GWh) · autonomy/software attach.

**Valuation:** core auto on P/E / EV/EBITDA vs peers (through-cycle margins); other segments via SOTP; autonomy/robotics as explicit option value (probability × payoff), not a multiple on hope.

**Traps:** regulatory credits in core EPS; price cuts to defend volume; capitalizing optionality into the core multiple.

## Industrials & capital goods

**Value equation:** Installed base × aftermarket revenue + new equipment orders through the cycle; ROIC spread over WACC.

**KPIs:** orders and book-to-bill · backlog and its margin · aftermarket/service % revenue · price vs cost (incl. tariffs) · through-cycle EBIT margin · ROIC · working-capital days · **contract liabilities (customer advances) vs revenue** — for project/engineered-to-order businesses, advances fund working capital and inflate cash at order peaks (balance sheet, "contract liabilities" or "customer deposits").

**Benchmarks:** book-to-bill ≥1.0; aftermarket mix raises through-cycle margins and multiples; ROIC >10–12% for quality.

**Valuation:** through-cycle EV/EBIT + ROIC-spread analysis; DCF with backlog-driven near-term revenue.

**Traps:** peak margins at the top of capex cycles; backlog cancellations; pension mark-to-market noise (R0); treating advance-inflated cash as excess cash (deduct excess float — valuation-core §8); sales-to-capital DCFs that turn an order downturn into a cash windfall; favorable percentage-of-completion estimate changes and project closeouts that flatter margins late in a cycle; the company's own prior cycle as the base rate (pull 10–15 years from older 10-Ks).

## Aerospace & defense

**Value equation (commercial aero):** Installed engines/aircraft × flight hours × aftermarket content; OE deliveries are often loss-leaders.
**Value equation (defense):** Funded backlog × program margin; program-of-record longevity.

**KPIs:** installed base and shop visits · departures/flight hours · OE delivery schedules (Boeing/Airbus) · defense budget appropriations · book-to-bill · fixed-price development exposure.

**Valuation:** DCF / P/E on aftermarket-driven earnings; defense on EV/EBIT and FCF yield.

**Traps:** fixed-price development charges (R1 — they keep happening); supply-chain constraints; continuing-resolution budget delays.

## Airlines

**Value equation:** Capacity (ASMs) × load factor × yield − CASM; extreme operating leverage.

**KPIs:** RASM vs CASM-ex fuel · load factor · fuel cost & hedging · premium/loyalty revenue (co-brand card economics) · fleet age and lease vs own · liquidity.

**Valuation:** mid-cycle EV/EBITDAR (lease-adjusted); loyalty program SOTP (valued like a payments business); asset value of fleet.

**Traps:** peak-cycle EPS; ignoring leases; labor contract step-ups.

## Railroads

**Value equation:** Carloads × revenue per carload − operating cost; an irreplicable network with near-duopoly pricing, so the efficiency lever is the **operating ratio** (operating expenses ÷ revenue).

**KPIs:** carloads/units by commodity (intermodal, coal, chemicals, autos, grain) · revenue per unit split into price, fuel surcharge and mix · operating ratio · train velocity, terminal dwell, on-time performance · capex % revenue · regulatory exposure (Surface Transportation Board) · coal secular decline.

**If undisclosed:** weekly carloads by railroad from AAR traffic reports; core pricing ex-fuel from management commentary ("same-store price"); fuel-surcharge lag from the diesel price path.

**Benchmarks:** best Class I operating ratios ~55–60%, laggards 65%+; capex 15–20% of revenue; leverage ~2.5–3x net debt/EBITDA.

**Valuation:** DCF and P/E on core EPS; EV/EBITDA vs Class I peers. The network can't be replicated, so a terminal RONIC above WACC is defensible — but state it and size it.

**Traps:** fuel-surcharge timing flattering or depressing margins (R3); land and asset-sale gains (R0); OR "improvement" from under-investment that later shows up as service failure and regulatory backlash; merger synergy targets.

## Trucking, LTL & logistics

**Value equation:**
- **LTL (less-than-truckload):** shipments × weight per shipment × revenue per hundredweight − cost of a dense terminal network. Network density is the moat.
- **Truckload:** loaded miles × rate per mile − driver, fuel and equipment cost. Fragmented and cyclical.
- **Freight brokerage:** loads × net revenue per load (gross margin) − people cost. Asset-light.
- **Parcel:** pieces × revenue per piece − network cost per piece.

**KPIs:** tonnage/shipments per day · revenue per hundredweight ex-fuel (LTL yield) · operating ratio · truckload contract vs spot rate per mile · tractor count and utilization · terminals and door count · net revenue margin and loads per employee (brokers) · average daily volume and cost per piece (parcel) · insurance and claims cost · gains on equipment sales · customer concentration (insourcing risk for parcel).

**If undisclosed:** freight-cycle position from the Cass Freight Index, ATA tonnage, DAT spot vs contract spread and Class 8 truck orders; broker gross margin squeeze when spot rates rise faster than contract rates.

**Benchmarks:** best LTL operating ratios ~70–75%, average ~80–85%; truckload mid-cycle OR ~88–92%; leverage <2x net debt/EBITDA through the cycle (truckers that lever up at peaks are the ones that fail).

**Valuation:** mid-cycle EPS × mid-cycle P/E or mid-cycle EV/EBITDA; brokers on net revenue, never gross; LTL network replacement cost (doors × cost per door) as a moat and floor check.

**Traps:** capitalizing peak freight-cycle earnings; equipment-sale gains in core EPS (R3); nuclear verdicts and insurance cost spikes; one-off share gains when a competitor fails (its terminals and freight get redistributed once); gross revenue growth at brokers that is only rate inflation; union contract step-ups at parcel carriers.

## Shipping (tankers, dry bulk, containers, gas carriers)

**Value equation:** NAV = fleet market value − net debt; earnings = vessel days × (time-charter-equivalent rate − vessel opex − G&A per day). Rates swing with the gap between fleet supply and trade demand.

**KPIs:** TCE rate per day vs cash breakeven per day · fleet age and fuel efficiency (eco/scrubber, IMO compliance) · orderbook as % of existing fleet · contract coverage (charter backlog and counterparty) · dividend policy and payout of FCF.

**If undisclosed:** vessel values from broker valuations (VesselsValue, Clarksons) by size and age; cash breakeven = (opex + G&A + interest + scheduled debt amortization) ÷ vessel days; forward rates from FFA curves where traded.

**Benchmarks:** orderbook above ~15–20% of the fleet = supply risk 2–3 years out; useful life ~25 years with scrap value at the end.

**Valuation:** P/NAV (shipping equities trade around NAV through the cycle; buy at a discount, not on yield); FCF yield at forward/FFA rates and at mid-cycle TCE; DCF on charter backlog for long-term contracted fleets (LNG carriers).

**Traps:** annualizing spot TCE; dividend yields computed on peak quarters; ignoring depreciation and fleet renewal capex; regulatory obsolescence of older tonnage; counterparty risk on long charters.

## Space & early-stage hardware

**Value equation:** Backlog conversion × launch/production cadence × unit margin, with heavy dilution and execution risk.

**KPIs:** backlog and its quality (government vs commercial) · launch cadence / production rate · cost per unit and gross margin trajectory · new-vehicle/program milestones · cash runway and dilution.

**Valuation:** scenario DCF (each scenario carries a survival/success probability) or year-5 revenue × target margin × exit multiple discounted back at 12–15%, dilution-adjusted; compare EV/backlog with peers.

**Traps:** linear extrapolation of milestones; underestimating dilution; treating announced contracts (options/IDIQ ceilings) as firm backlog.
